Section A: Short calculations

1. A firm sells 400 units at £15 each. Calculate total revenue. (2)
2. Total revenue is £300 when 10 units are sold and £330 when 11 units are sold. Calculate marginal revenue. (2)
3. A firm has total fixed costs of £2,000 and average variable cost of £5 when it produces 500 units. Calculate total cost and average cost. (3)
4. Total cost rises from £1,200 at 100 units to £1,215 at 101 units. Calculate marginal cost. (2)
5. A firm sells 800 units at £12 each. Its total cost is £8,200. Calculate its profit and its profit margin to 1 d.p. (4)
6. The price of a product rises from £8 to £10 and quantity demanded falls from 500 to 425. Calculate PED, and state the effect on total revenue. (4)
7. Profit falls from £50,000 to £42,000. Calculate the percentage change in profit. (2)
8. A firm's AR is £6, its AVC is £5.50 and its AC is £7. Should it continue producing in the short run? Should it stay in the market in the long run? Explain. (4)

Answers

3.2-3.3 Calculations Worksheet

  1. TR = P × Q = 15 × 400 = £6,000
  2. MR = change in TR ÷ change in Q = (330 − 300) ÷ 1 = £30
  3. TVC = 5 × 500 = £2,500; TC = 2,000 + 2,500 = £4,500; AC = 4,500 ÷ 500 = £9
  4. MC = (1,215 − 1,200) ÷ 1 = £15
  5. TR = 12 × 800 = £9,600; profit = 9,600 − 8,200 = £1,400; margin = 1,400 ÷ 9,600 × 100 = 14.6%
  6. %ΔP = 2 ÷ 8 × 100 = +25%; %ΔQ = −75 ÷ 500 × 100 = −15%; PED = −15 ÷ 25 = −0.6 (inelastic). TR rises from £4,000 to £4,250, because a price rise on inelastic demand raises revenue.
  7. (42,000 − 50,000) ÷ 50,000 × 100 = −16%
  8. Continue in the short run: AR (£6) is above AVC (£5.50), so it covers variable costs and contributes £0.50 per unit towards fixed costs. In the long run it should leave: AR (£6) is below AC (£7), so it makes a loss of £1 per unit and cannot cover all costs.

  1. Q = 3, profit £18. At 3 units MR (£12) is above MC (£8), so the third unit adds to profit. The fourth unit has MC (£10) above MR (£8), so it reduces profit. Profit is highest at 3 units.
  2. Q = 5 or 6 (TR is £60 at both). MR from the sixth unit is £0, so revenue stops rising at that point; revenue is at its maximum where MR reaches zero.
  3. Q = 5. At 5 units AR (£12) is above AC (£10.80); at 6 units AR (£10) is below AC (£12), a loss. This is sales maximisation (output where AR ≈ AC, with no loss).
  4. Profit maximisation: price £16 at 3 units. Sales maximisation: price £12 at 5 units. Price is £4 lower (25% lower) under sales maximisation, and output is higher.
